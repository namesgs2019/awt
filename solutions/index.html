Water Reuse & Phosphorus Removal

The conventional process for water reuse — purifying treated wastewater and converting it into industrial-grade water — generally relies on chemical coagulation followed by membrane filtration and reverse osmosis (RO). This has been the standard method not only in Korea but also globally.

However, the use of chemical coagulants significantly increases the fouling load on the subsequent RO stage. As a result, the efficiency of the RO process deteriorates, the volume of brine generated increases, and overall water-reuse efficiency declines sharply.

AWT provides a superior alternative by eliminating chemical dosing. Instead, contaminants are selectively agglomerated through electro-coagulation, and then filtered using high-durability ceramic membranes, creating an RO-optimized feedwater condition.

This approach minimizes brine generation, maximizes RO performance, and ultimately delivers significantly higher water-reuse efficiency.

Conventional Phosphorus-Removal Method and Its Limitations

Conventional phosphorus-removal processes often depend on alum or PAC dosing, which can increase sludge generation and leave residual ions such as sulfate and chloride in the system.

Portable Facility

This is a modular water treatment plant capable of supplying more than 400 tons of potable water per day. The entire system is integrated into a single 40ft container, making transportation and installation extremely easy.

We are currently in discussions to supply each unit for USD 1 million to Canada, Turkmenistan, Malaysia, and other regions.

The system can be combined with solar power, offering broad applicability for islands, remote areas, resorts, and standalone facilities requiring reliable drinking-water supply.

If required, a reverse osmosis (RO) module can be added, and in areas with high water hardness, a zeolite filtration system can also be incorporated.

Vessel-Integrated Ceramic Membrane Module

We have optimized the system by integrating the world's highest-performance flatsheet-type ceramic membrane into a vessel-integrated module.

The ceramic membrane delivers a clean-water permeability of 3,000 L/m²·hr·bar, which is significantly superior to Meidensha of Japan (1,700 L/m²·hr·bar) and Chinese products (1,000-1,500 L/m²·hr·bar).

The automatic air backwash process further maximizes operational efficiency.

As a vessel-integrated, side-stream design, the system offers extremely easy installation, expansion, maintenance, and operation.

AWT is currently seeking mass-production partners for its Vessel-Integrated Ceramic Membrane Module.
